# Orb: independent whole-product UI/UX review

27 October 2026 · 466 × 466 round AMOLED · simulator evidence, not a physical-panel sign-off

## Verdict

**It does not yet feel as though Apple made the whole product.** It has a credible watchOS vocabulary, unusually good foundations for this hardware, and several genuinely elegant interactions. But navigation, reading order, accessibility reflow and feedback do not yet form one dependable system. The Face is recognisable; Home's bezel controls have a reason to exist; the games still look like engineering demonstrations. The largest improvement will come from making the product coherent, not adding more material effects.

I would not pass the current product through its own design gate. Two issues should block a release: celebrating an answer before delivery is confirmed, and hiding a destructive action's consequence at the largest text size. Most remaining problems are bounded craft and interaction tasks.

## Evidence and limits

I read `HANDOFF.md`, `hig.md`, `delight.md`, `home-app.md`, the navigation model in `design.md`, the mandatory design gate, and the polish backlog; checked the protocol/voice context and physical-panel feedback; and inspected the relevant implementation. Two independent agents checked coverage and interaction semantics. **I personally inspected every captured image**, using paired contact sheets for settled surfaces, every frame of the recorded sequences, and native-size originals for consequential findings.

Evidence is under [screens/ux-review](screens/ux-review/). Start with the [visual index](screens/ux-review/index.html), [per-surface evidence](screens/ux-review/gate-evidence.md), [capture commands and output](screens/ux-review/capture-log.json), and [image manifest](screens/ux-review/manifest.json). The manifest records dimensions, hashes and contact-sheet membership. The two principal profiles are:

- **D:** Default text, ordinary weight, normal transparency and motion.
- **A:** AX3 + Bold Text + Reduce Transparency, with motion left enabled. Combining Reduce Motion with this profile would conceal motion/layout failures, so it was not silently added.

The corpus contains **920 native PNGs**: all 95 named shell previews, all 49 named Home previews, real pointer flows, 202 shell sequence frames per profile, 50 Home sequence frames per profile, all four games at initial/playing/result/save-error states, game pause/large-score/failure combinations, the otherwise unnamed Connection and confirmation screens, seven legacy renderer moments per profile, and five empty states per profile. Aliases and synthetic fixture names are identified as such; they are not extra product destinations.

The requested CMake command initially failed because this checkout lacks managed cJSON sources. An unchanged local copy was staged in `sim/build/review-deps`; a build-only CMake include redirects that dependency. No firmware source or source CMake file was edited. Configuration and the full simulator build subsequently passed, including the exact requested command using that cached include. Ten focused existing checks passed: shell contract, delight contract, game rendering/logic/runtime, Home view at Default/AX3/Reduce Motion, and Home shell integration at Default/AX3. Passing those checks does not establish design quality.

Large PNG contact-sheet previews intermittently omitted ink that was present in the files. I checked the originals and added JPEG viewing copies. **There is no missing-clock or missing-Orbit-score finding in this review.** Use original PNGs for pixel measurements and JPEG sheets for browsing.

These captures cannot establish finger accuracy on the glass, real sound quality, the recording-start click, panel tint/fringing, perceived brightness, TE/frame pacing, or device FPS. The shell's `face-always-on` fixture stops ambient effects; it does not implement or validate a complete dimmed/redacted Always On policy. The shell's registered-Marble motion fixture proves registry launch/exit and displays an active-app placeholder; the separate game executable renders the actual four games. A shell-to-real-game end-to-end handoff is not demonstrated by those placeholder frames. A long-PWR power menu has no implemented simulator surface to review.

## Cross-cutting findings

### 1. The five system destinations need five distinct jobs

| Destination | Product role to make explicit | Current friction | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face | Time and the few things needing attention now | Unlabelled bell/up-chevron shortcuts; generic “Mac”; a battery label competes with attention; agent arcs have no readable quantitative explanation | Keep time dominant. Make complications name their destination or state. The Face should answer “Do I need to do anything?” |
| Apps | Stable catalogue of capabilities | Only Agents has a persistent caption at Default; AX3 repeats “Open app”; a static caption can appear to describe the whole cluster | Name the app under the finger. Preserve positions and return focus. Keep the accessible list. |
| Smart Stack | Relevance-ordered live work | The approval surface and a choice card can look like whole apps; card paging and text scrolling are separate but their visible controls do not explain that | One card per page, stable source identity, truthful page position, explicit deeper-reading disclosure. |
| Notifications | History and recent events | Similar card content appears here and in Stack, without visible timestamps; “Clear All” competes with reading the next item | Use source + time. Clearing presentation must continue to leave pending decisions intact. |
| Control Center | Immediate device/home controls | Called “Controls”; Home is an undisclosed extra page; a whole AX3 page goes to Reduce Motion while Silent and Home are further away | Keep daily controls first. Give every page accurate navigation; do not make a persistent accessibility preference feel like a main product destination. |

Duplication is acceptable when a complication or notification is a shortcut to **the same object with the same state and return path**. It becomes confusing when changing the entry point changes the apparent task or hides the user's previous context. Use one noun for each destination in UI, documentation and accessibility descriptions.

### 2. Navigation and toolbars: the individual controls are better than the model

The back chevron, diagonal glass circles and explicit confirmation controls are sound. The mistakes are in what those controls mean:

- The shell's rim ellipsis at **(431,233), 46 × 72 px** means scroll farther, change Stack card, or change a setting. At a list's end it silently jumps back to the top (`orb_workspace.c`, `ACT_NEXT`). An ellipsis conventionally promises more actions, not movement through space.
- Home's top-right chevron first advances accessories, then changes Favourites/Rooms/Climate; on Rooms it first scrolls. Horizontal swipes change sections immediately. The supposed visible gesture alternative does not reliably do the same thing.
- Home detail Back skips the room grid: Rooms → Living Room → Lamp → Back lands at Rooms. This is source-established in `back_event()` in `orb_home_view.c`.
- AX3 Control Center cycles **four** pages but draws **three** dots. The Home page draws none. Default has two pages with no equivalent indicator.

Adopt three separate contracts: chevron = Back; downward continuation = more content in this view; section/page control = a named neighbouring page. Preserve scroll offset, selected accessory and app focus on Back. Use X for a modal cancellation/exit, never as an interchangeable Back. A voice cancellation must not feel like discarding a session.

### 3. Typography: the font is good; hierarchy is not yet systematic

Inter is a strong choice. Tabular clock/counters, heavier accessible text and the licensing discipline should stay. The problem is that page titles are demoted to Footnote 2 when their measured ink exceeds a fixed 188 px lane. “Accessibility” and “Notifications” therefore become less important because their names do not fit. This is visible in the captures and explicit in `header()`; reflow should solve it before semantic demotion.

Use the semantic scale in `hig.md`: Body/Headline **28/32 px**, Title 3 **33/37**, supporting Footnote 1 **23/27** and Footnote 2 **21/25** at D; their actual AX3/Bold variants at A. Do not squeeze A back into D geometry. At A, one complete session row can be the correct density. An unreadable second row is not an improvement.

Reserve three text roles: a compact destination title, a clear task/value, and supporting context. Remove redundant “Voice / mic / mic / Listening” or “Home / Open Home / Open Home” repetitions. Essential consequences, commands and recipient names must wrap or scroll, never disappear behind ellipsis. State words should not be demoted just because source metadata has expanded.

### 4. Circle, spacing and resting composition

Round-list scaling and edge dissolution are substantial achievements. However, `agents-next` at A settles with the next session's title already cut by the top fade, while its project and last step remain in the centre. This reverses reading order. The peripheral title-only previews also look like disconnected floating labels in Settings and Sessions preferences. Curvature should explain depth and continuity, not detach a row's parts.

Use an **8 px layout rhythm**, with 16/24/32 px content gaps and the mandated **20 px control-to-control gap**. Keep toolbar centres **(101,101)/(365,101)** and normal targets **72 px**. A proposed common shell layout is:

- Optional compact time centred **(233,44)**, Footnote 2; short destination title in a **188 px** central lane near **y=82**. Long AX3 titles move to a complete content heading below the toolbar rather than being reduced to footnotes.
- Reading content begins around **y=148**. When there is a bottom primary, finish the readable region by **y=350**, leaving clear space before a **72 px** capsule centred **(233,399)**.
- Every line uses the narrower chord at its top **and** bottom, with **16 px text** and **10 px material** rim insets. These are constraints, not permission to fit a fixed 346 px rectangle everywhere.
- A settled list must put the first complete readable row below the top fade. It may show a genuinely peripheral preview above it, but must not strand that row's metadata as the principal content.

Black empty space is valuable around a single temperature or decision. It is wasteful when a button has advanced only two-thirds of a viewport and left the next thing half hidden. Solve resting positions before tightening every gap.

### 5. Colour and glass: more discipline, not more grey

The black canvas, restrained fills, flat app discs, and small state accents are broadly right. Home's actual light colours and the launcher catalogue are meaningful exceptions to the two-accent rule; turning those into a uniform grey interface would lose information and the owner's requested delight. Source-colour neutralisation in mixed agent lists is deliberate, not a missing implementation: the current two-accent budget prioritises orange plus error/completion/one source. Preserve source glyph shapes and text whenever a hue is neutralised.

The current weakness is semantic instability. A source may become grey because a different session finishes; the same generic “Attention” header uses a Dot-like bubble for a doorbell. Put source identity in a stable location and use a doorbell/house glyph for Home events. Reserve red for actual error/recording meaning, green for confirmed success, and orange for attention. Do not add tinted controls to make sparse screens look finished.

Glass is generally used for navigation and action rather than lists or cards. That is excellent. On this predominantly black product it can look like a dark outlined button; extra sheen would not fix that. Preserve the material's lift, quiet rim and real content dependence. Remove redundant glass controls before increasing blur. Test press/return under both normal and opaque settings; screenshot appearance alone does not establish adaptive backdrop contrast or moving highlights on the panel.

There are real standards conflicts to resolve explicitly:

- The gate's “white app glyph” shorthand conflicts with `hig.md` §5 contrast on yellow/orange/green. **HIG wins. Keep adaptive black/white ink.**
- The Home sketch's seven-disc aspiration is subordinate to target size and spacing. **Keep the current five-disc pagination** where it preserves 72 px targets and 20 px separation.
- “No glow anywhere” in the mandatory gate is stricter than the older HIG rendering paragraph proposing a touch glow. **Do not add a new glow.** Use geometric press response and a material/rim change.
- The two-accent rule needs a documented launcher/accessory-state exception, already reflected in the implementation. Do not apply it indiscriminately to the app catalogue or actual lamp colours.

### 6. Feedback and motion: a truth problem before a delight problem

The most serious finding is directly visible in `approve-queued-*`: a green fill and check appear while the screen says **“Queued · Waiting for your Mac.”** The code plays the approval/denial sound as soon as `orb_model_answer()` accepts the local queue entry. Acknowledged delivery is checked later. Delight currently claims an outcome that has not happened.

Use a neutral press and “Sending answer…” for the local state. Celebrate only authoritative successful delivery. A denied command that was delivered successfully is a successful communication, not a red error; its final text should say “Denied” or “Denial sent,” without implying the device failed. Keep no-auto-replay for ambiguous outcomes.

There is also a real A-layout defect: the approval check capsule is fixed at **centre y=313**, directly over “Waiting for your Mac.” (`card_receipt()`, 250 × 72 at A). It must live in reserved space or within normal layout flow.

The live microphone level ring, hold-to-unlock progress/cancellation, toggle fill and count changes are purposeful. Keep them. The app-launch sequence spends roughly the 48–176 ms samples behind an almost solid accent disc, with readable content arriving later. The origin relationship is pleasing; the opaque colour hold is too dominant for an otherwise quiet object. Let content become legible by about **150 ms**, reduce the fully opaque interval, and keep the launch interruptible. This is a design timing target, not a measured input-latency claim.

Reduce Motion must replace spatial launch/reward/count movement with the specified 150 ms fade, not merely shorten it. Existing focused checks pass; this review's two main profiles intentionally retain motion, so that is not a separate all-surface visual certification of Reduce Motion. The physical recording-start click remains a board backlog item, not something these PNGs can clear.

### 7. Accessibility and copy are product structure

AX3/Bold/RT is functional on many surfaces, but not consistently composed. The important failures are the destructive confirmation, overlapping receipt stamp, clipped resting list title, crowded Climate view, and many names truncated before the item is identifiable. There is no demonstrated screen-reader/spoken-focus surface in this simulator; these font and contrast preferences should not be described as complete accessibility support.

Use plain owner-facing language: “Mac” rather than “hub,” “Other sessions” rather than a separate app-like “Idle,” “Last finished 4 min ago” rather than “Outside focus” where the distinction is really recency. Preserve exact technical commands where the user is reviewing a command. Do not rewrite command content into a friendly summary that loses scope. Developer setup commands are appropriate to the current Mac CLI onboarding, but should say **“In Terminal on your Mac, run:”** and label the displayed number as the setup/pairing code.

## What is genuinely excellent and must stay

1. **The round-display premise is real.** Curved lists, circular progress and bezel input are structurally better than a miniature phone UI.
2. **Home's rim drag, ± alternative, touch-only knob, deliberate lock hold and cancellation.** This is the product's best differentiated interaction. Keep five-disc pagination rather than trading touch safety for a prettier static seven-disc picture.
3. **Approval restraint.** Equal untinted decisions, persistent Deny, exact command/scope, full reading and no decision timeout. The celebration bug must not prompt a redesign into a one-tap green approval UI.
4. **Voice agency.** Real level response, visible mic, complete transcript, explicit Send, persistent Cancel and no timed sending. Keep the fixed no-speech retry wording.
5. **Honest failure distinctions.** Queued/Recorded/Sent/Not sent and unknown delivery, no silent replay; Home rollback; disabled stale controls; update safety reassurance. Make animation agree with this excellent model.
6. **True black, flat identity discs, restrained palette, Inter and Framework7-style glyphs.** Keep the licensing decisions and adaptive glyph ink. More decoration would dilute these strengths.
7. **The existing evidence infrastructure.** Deterministic real-pointer captures and AX3/opaque variants make product judgement possible. Keep the native originals; do not equate a passing renderer test with a passing design review.
